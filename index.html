The essentials

Restrained, monochrome, and proven with evidence.

Peace Intel speaks to people who decide where money, staff and trust go in peace and security work. They are not shouted at. The brand earns trust through restraint: serif claims, quiet sans body text, mono labels, hairlines instead of shadows, and ink as the strongest emphasis. If you remember eight things, make it these.

  1. Paper and ink do almost all the work. Warm off-white pages, near-black type. Orange is a small, deliberate signal.
  2. Orange means something. It marks evidence, the main action, or a live state. Three or four orange spots per slide at most, and never as decoration.
  3. Serif for claims, sans for reading, mono for labels. Source Serif 4 headlines in Regular, never bold. Barlow body. Space Mono in small capitals for labels.
  4. Big jumps in size. Headlines run about four times the body size. Hierarchy comes from size and space, not weight.
  5. Dark sections are the loudest move. Use a dark band for the one claim that matters, the close, or a key number. Never two dark slides back to back.
  6. One loud thing at a time. One dove, one picture or one animation per slide. Imagery is always monochrome and pixel-dithered or plain greyscale.
  7. Every corner is 4px. Nothing casts a shadow. Cards are lifted by a 1px edge. No gradients, no glows.
  8. Numbers and sources over adjectives. Say what the evidence shows and how strong it is. No em dashes, no hype, no invented figures.
Two things to check with Matt before anything is printed: the corpus figures (the current home page uses 258,621 papers; an earlier page says 5,000+ curated sources) and the event date line, if one is used. The live app address and sign-up destination are also not final, so leave them off or use a placeholder.

05 · Copy

Approved lines, ready to paste.

All of this is live on the current home page, so it is safe to use. American English throughout. Each line has a copy button.

Voice

Prove with evidence, never with adjectives.

A number, a source or a stated limit does more than “powerful” or “revolutionary.”

State the strength of every claim.

Say when evidence is strong, mixed or untested. A small effect stays small.

Restraint is the register.

Calm, exact, institutional. The reader decides budgets and policy and does not want to be sold to.

Say

Every source is vetted and ranked for quality, and every claim carries its citation.

Avoid

Revolutionary AI that transforms peacebuilding forever!

  • No em dashes in any copy. Use a comma, a colon or a full stop.
  • No “not this, but that” constructions. Say what it is.
  • No testimonials, customer names or pricing. None exist yet. Never invent them.
  • No invented numbers. Use only the corpus figures below.

The problem with general chat assistants

Praise before scrutiny. A plan is complimented before it is tested, so a weak assumption comes back sounding sound.
Answers that bend. Push back or reframe the question, and the answer moves toward what you wanted to hear.
Evidence stretched thin. One small study in one place becomes “research shows.”
Guesses in place of questions. Missing context is filled with assumptions, and the answer never says so.

What Peace Intel does instead

Pushes back on weak premises. When a plan rests on a shaky assumption, it says so, and holds that view when pressed.
Reports each source at its true strength. A small effect stays small, and every claim carries its citation.
Names the gaps. It says what the evidence does not cover, and asks for the context it needs.
Shows what each answer rests on. Every source is marked by how much of it was read: the full paper, the abstract, or the open web.

Pictures

The greyscale illustrations used across the home page. The originals are small (about 700px), fine at half a slide. For anything larger, use the dithered version: the same picture redrawn in the brand’s pixel grid at 2880px, which enlarges cleanly.

Original or dithered

Same picture, two treatments. The original reads as a soft illustration; the dithered one matches the veil and holds up on a big screen or in print.

Rules for imagery

  1. One image per slide. One dove, one picture or one loop. Never two competing.
  2. Always monochrome. Ink on paper, or paper on dark. Never tinted orange or coloured.
  3. Enlarge pixels with hard edges. In Photoshop choose Nearest Neighbor. Smooth resampling blurs the dither.
  4. The veil is atmosphere. It can sit beside or behind a headline, but type never crosses the dense part of the bird.
  5. No texture over imagery. The grid texture and a picture never share the same area.
  6. Let the pictures float. Show them on the page colour with no frame, box or drop shadow.
Graphic devices

The small things that make it look like Peace Intel.

The grid texture

A dashed 20px grid, very faint, that always fades out at its edges. It sits behind hero areas and stats. Never on two neighbouring sections, never under a picture. The web strength nearly vanishes on a projector, so there is a 3× version for the big screen.
